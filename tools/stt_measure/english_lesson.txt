Let us look at the Italian Game and at the Evans Gambit that grows out of it.
White opens with the king's pawn, e4, and Black answers e5.
The knight comes to f3 and attacks the pawn, and the knight on c6 defends it.
Now the bishop goes to c4, where it looks at the weak square f7.
Black develops his own bishop to c5.
Here White offers a pawn: b4, the Evans Gambit.
After bishop takes b4, White plays c3 and wins a tempo.
Black has three retreats: bishop to a5, bishop to e7, and bishop back to c5.
We will look at bishop c5.
White strikes in the centre with d4, and after the pawn is taken he castles kingside.
If Black is greedy and takes on c3, the queen threatens to come to d5.
From d5 the queen attacks the bishop and threatens checkmate on f7.
Black defends both with queen to e7.
So White does better to sacrifice the bishop on f7 at once.
After the king takes, the queen gives check on d5 and wins the bishop on c5.
