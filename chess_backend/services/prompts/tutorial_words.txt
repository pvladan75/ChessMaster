# Write the words for a chess tutorial, as the story of a game

A trainer's game is being turned into a tutorial video for a student of 13 or
older: the board plays the moves, and every sentence is read aloud one after
the other. The student plays in a club and knows how the pieces move and what
a fork and a pin are.

**Everything except the words is already done**, by a program and a chess
engine: every position, every move and every line. None of it can change. You
do two things.

1. **Choose** two or three of the moments below - the ones a student learns the
   most from - by their ids. Prefer the moments the story of the game turns on.
2. **Write** a title (under 60 characters), a one-sentence description, one or
   two tags, and the text of **every slot of the moments you chose**, and of no
   other slot.

## The voice

A game of chess is a fight, and the tutorial tells it. Everything you write is
heard in order, together with a few sentences the program adds, as **one story**:
where the fight stands, who is pressing, the chance one side gives the other,
what was taken or missed, and what it cost.

- **The board plays each move while its slot is read.** Never announce the move
  („Black plays Qc7") and never describe where a piece goes („the queen from d8
  to c7") - the student is watching it happen. Say what the move means: what it
  attacks, defends, prepares, allows or gives away.
- **Name a move in notation only when it is not the move of this slot.**
- **Carry on from the slot before.** Do not open two slots with the same words,
  and do not start every sentence with a colour.
- **A move slot of a lead-in or of a line may be an empty string** ("") when the
  move needs no words; the board waits over it in silence. Leave at most half of
  a part's move slots empty, and never the first move of a best line.
- **Never write a sentence whose only content is that the line carries on.**
  „The line goes on", „and the line continues", „this is how the line goes" say
  nothing a student can use, and they read as the same sentence four times over.
  If a move has nothing to tell, its slot is the empty string.
- Short, concrete sentences in the present tense.

## Rules for every sentence

- **Say only what the facts beside the slot say.** Do not judge a move yourself.
  A move wins material only if its facts say it captures something and the
  material count bears it out; a fork or a pin exists only if the facts name it.
- **No numbers for evaluations.** The facts turn them into words; use the words.
- **A move slot is about that one move**, not the move after it.
- **Keep what happened apart from what should have.** A slot marked „not played"
  is a move the game did not contain: say it as a line that was there, never as
  something that happened.
- One or two short sentences per slot, at most 140 characters.
- Teach, do not score: say what to notice, not only that a move was bad.
