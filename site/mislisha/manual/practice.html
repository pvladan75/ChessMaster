Practise on your own

The Training tab is a list of things to work at, arranged by phase of the game: your own play first, then tactics, then endgames and technique. None of it needs a trainer.

My games

Your own games, and what went wrong in them. Import games takes a PGN export from wherever you play; Include online games and Import more games add to the pile. Out of them the app builds several views: Game mistakes, Opening leaks — where your opening keeps letting something in — Profile and habits, and Repertoire from games, which turns what you actually play into the start of a repertoire.

My mistakes

The positions where you went wrong, handed back to you as exercises. The app remembers when a mistake was made and brings the position round again later, so the same error gets harder to repeat. Judge moves and Punish ask you to say what was wrong and then to show it.

Opening repertoire

Not puzzles but decisions: what you would play, position by position, kept and rehearsed. It has a page of its own — building a repertoire.

Tactics tailored to you

Puzzles from the Lichess database, chosen by your rating and by the motif you are weakest at; your rating is kept per motif rather than as one number, so the pins keep coming until the pins are solid. Hint and Show solution are there when a position will not give.

Puzzles: Mate in 1, 2 or 3 moves

Solve forced checkmate sequences in the requested number of moves. Choose the depth and work through them; the answer is a forced mate, not the best move.

Endgames from master games

Endgames as they actually occurred, taken from grandmaster games. Filters narrow them to the kind you want to drill — for instance Opposite-colored bishops only. In a position you can Play to the end against the engine, ask for a Hint, or Start over, and where the point is a draw rather than a win, Conclude draw. On a computer the letters shown on the endgame screen do the same as its buttons.

Practice basic checkmates

Checkmate the opponent in classic mating positions against Stockfish. The engine defends properly, which is what makes queen and king against king worth practising at all.

Find the winning path

Play winning positions out against Stockfish with optional Blunder Alert. You are given a position that is won and have to convert it; with the alert on, the app stops you at the move that throws it away instead of letting the game drift.

What comes back later

Much of what you solve here returns on its own, spaced out over days, in Review on the Sessions tab. That is where a mistake you drilled last week turns up again to see whether it stuck.
