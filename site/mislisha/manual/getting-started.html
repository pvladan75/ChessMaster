Find your way around

The app is four tabs and a settings screen. This page says what each of them is for, so the rest of the manual can get on with the work.

Sign in, or look first

The first screen offers three ways in:

  • Sign in with email — your address and password. Remember me keeps you signed in on this device.
  • Sign in / Register with Google — no separate password.
  • Continue as Guest — straight in, without an account.

Registering with an address instead uses Register with email, and the app may ask for a code it has mailed you: Email Verification, Enter Verification Code, then Confirm Verification. Back to sign in returns to the first screen.

What a guest cannot do: start or join a session, and save analysis. The app does not refuse at the end of the job — it takes you to the sign-in screen and, once you are in, carries on with what you were doing.

The year you were born

Once, before anything else, the app asks for Birth year — the year only — and Save puts it away. Sign out is there for anyone who would rather not answer.

Two things follow from the year. Mislisha is for players of thirteen and over, and a younger year is refused rather than quietly stored. And in countries where the age of consent for a service like this is higher than thirteen, a student below it needs a parent's confirmation before a trainer's invitation takes effect; the parent gets a link by mail, and until they answer the connection waits.

The four tabs

Down the left on a wide window, along the bottom on a narrow one.

Training — practice on your own

A list of what there is to work at: your own games and the mistakes in them, an opening repertoire, tactics, mates in one to three, endgames, basic checkmates and winning positions to convert. Nothing here needs another person.

Sessions — the live lesson, and homework

New Session starts a room as host; Enter room code (e.g. 123456) joins one. Preparation opens the same board alone, with your own material. Below them are My assignments — what a trainer has set you — Review, which brings positions back when their time comes, and Recorded material.

Library — your material

Interactive tutorials for writing and opening tutorials, My saved positions, Open Analysis for the engine and the tree, and Scan positions, which takes diagrams out of a chess book in PDF form.

People — students and trainers

Sending and answering requests, My students and My trainers, Student groups, and the way into a student's progress.

The top bar

  • The bell opens Notifications and Invitations: requests to teach or be taught, and invitations to a session, each with Accept and Decline.
  • The gear opens Settings. On a wide window it is at the foot of the left-hand strip.
  • A session you walked away from keeps a band across the top with its code in it, and Resume session takes you back to the board.

What is in Settings

The screen is called App Settings, and holds, in order:

  • The voice. Speak messages turns reading aloud on, then the voice itself, a Test button, and Check for voices again for when you have just installed one in the operating system.
  • Appearance — light or dark.
  • Your account — Log out, Birth year, and Parent email where one is needed.
  • The engine — Configure local engine for Stockfish.
  • The board — its colours, the piece set, Board coordinates, and which panels the analysis screen shows.
  • Help — User manual, this manual, and Keyboard shortcuts.
  • Opening explorer — which database to ask, and a Lichess token if you have one.

Keys

On a computer, F1 opens the whole list of shortcuts; Ctrl+, opens Settings, and Ctrl+1 to Ctrl+4 go to the four tabs. Wherever a row of moves sits under a board, the arrow keys walk it. No shortcut is the only way to do anything.

If something is not where this page says

  • The tutorial card is missing from Library — writing a tutorial is a Windows feature; the tab does not draw that card elsewhere.
  • Nothing is read aloud — check Speak messages, and whether your device has a voice for the language; a tutorial that says which language it is written in is never read in another one.
  • A button asks you to sign in — you are in as a guest.
