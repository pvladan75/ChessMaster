Write a tutorial

A tutorial is a lesson a student walks through on their own: a line you play on the board, what you say about each position, what you draw on it, and questions along the way.

The Tutorial Studio is part of the Windows app. On a phone the Library tab has no tutorials card — write on Windows; your students can walk the tutorial on any device.

Start one

Open the Library tab. On the Interactive tutorials card:

  • New tutorial — give it a Tutorial title and press Create;
  • Saved tutorials — reopen one you have saved;
  • Import from a file — open a tutorial prepared outside the app.

Two words: beat and part

A beat is one moment: a position, what you say about it, and what is drawn on it. Every move you play on the board makes the next beat.

A part is one kind of task on one starting position. A part has as many beats as the moves you play in it.

The rule that saves the most work: play the moves on the board, and start a new part only when the kind of task changes or you jump to another position. A part for every move is ten times the work, and the student gets ten boards instead of one story.

Write a demonstration

  1. Play the line on the board.
  2. In the Flow tab every beat is a card. Click one and type into Comment for current move.

A sentence belongs to its beat and is read before the move that leaves it: the student sees the position, hears what you say about it, and then the next move is played. Something to say „after a move" is the sentence of the next beat.

The Tree tab shows the same line with its variations, and the PGN tab shows the part as text — and takes text in: paste an annotated game with its comments, arrows and coloured squares, and press Apply. The small icon on a beat's card, Delete this move, takes back the move that arrived there, with everything after it.

Draw on the board

The bar under the board has Arrow, Square, the colours, and Clear marks.

  • an arrow is two clicks, from and to; the same two clicks remove it;
  • a square is one click; another click removes it;
  • moving to another beat turns drawing off, so your first click there moves a piece again.

Ask a question

A question is always about the position a part starts on. You do not build that by hand: stand on the beat you want to ask about, and in Tutorial contents press Find the move or Choose the answer. The studio cuts the part for you — the demonstration stays in front, the question becomes a new part on this position, and whatever followed carries on after it. The student sees all of it on one board.

  • Find the move — the move that followed in your line is the answer. At the end of a line, play the answer on the board.
  • Choose the answer — write two to four answers with Add answer, and mark exactly one as correct.

What the student is asked goes in Task for student. The kind of a part can also be changed in Task type: Show only, Ask for move on board or Ask for answer from list.

A question never carries a line. The student can step through a part's moves, so a question with moves in it hands them the answer. The studio warns you when you are about to make one, and will not save one.

Show another line from the same position

Your demonstration goes one way and you want to show a second line from the middle of it. In Flow, stand on the beat where the lines part, and press the branching icon on its card, Insert a line here. The part becomes three: up to this beat, a new line from here — you are left on its first position, ready to play it — and the original continuation. Everything you wrote and drew stays where it was. If you had already played the second line as a variation, it becomes the new line by itself.

Start the next example

New demonstration asks Where does it start?: From here carries on from where the open part's line ends, and the student's board does not reload; New board starts from the opening position. To set up any other position, use Position setup, the sliders icon at the top.

Name it and say its language

Next to Tutorial title are Labels, which let you find a tutorial later, and Language. The language decides which voice reads the tutorial aloud on the student's device, and moves in your sentences are spoken in that language. Not set leaves it to the voice chosen in the reader's own settings.

Check it, then save it

Preview tutorial opens the tutorial the way a student walks it — buttons, voice and questions. Nothing is sent anywhere and no answer is recorded. It is not the look of the video: for that, open Export video and press its Preview button.

Save tutorial saves once, at the end. Every later press updates the same tutorial rather than making a new one. A tutorial needs a title, and the studio will not save a question that carries a line, or a list question without two to four answers and exactly one correct — it says which part.

Changed your mind?

  • Undo (Ctrl+Z) and Redo (Ctrl+Y) are the two arrows at the top. The studio remembers your last hundred changes for as long as it is open. A sentence typed without a pause is one change, not one per letter, and Ctrl+Z works with the cursor in a text field too.
  • Discard changes, beside them, goes back to the version you last saved — and is itself one undo away.
  • Work in progress is kept on this computer. Open a saved tutorial that has unsaved changes here, and the studio asks: Continue with my changes or Open the saved version. Neither loses anything while the studio is open.
  • Start a new tutorial while an unfinished one is waiting, and it asks about that one by name — You have an unfinished tutorial. Choosing a new one there deletes the unfinished one.
