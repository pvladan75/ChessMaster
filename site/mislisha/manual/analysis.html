Analyse a position or a game

One position, looked at properly: the engine's opinion, what masters have played, and a tree of the variations you try. It is also where a line you like becomes part of a tutorial.

Open it

Analysis is the Analyse tab — the board is the tab itself. Its bar carries Saved analyses and Scan a book among its tools — on a phone, behind ⋮.

Get a position onto the board

  • Board Setup puts pieces where you want them, with the side to move and Castling: rights; Clear board empties it first, and Erase takes single pieces off.
  • A game can be pasted or opened as a PGN file on the PGN tab, or fetched from a Chess.com or Lichess account on the Online tab with Download Games.
  • Anything in your library — a saved position, a game of your own — can be loaded onto the board.

The engine

Stockfish runs on your own machine, and the panel beside the board shows what it thinks and the lines it is considering. Which panels you see is your choice, under Panels in the board's toolbar; the engine itself is configured in Settings, with Configure local engine for a Custom local engine (.exe) if you have one.

Review entire game walks a whole game instead of one position, and Blunder detection is what marks the moments worth talking about. It can be told Analyze only from the current position forward when the opening is not the point.

Study one position

Study this position is for the opposite question: one position, gone through the way a strong player would talk you through it. The engine works out the main line, the moves that are as good as the best, the moves the eye goes to and why they are not the move, and the traps on the way. A trap is a capture that wins material and loses the position; it is marked with a question mark and followed by what punishes it, so the answer to „why not just take it?" is on the board. Everything is added to the tree under the move you are standing on, and nothing you wrote there is replaced.

With Write comments with AI ticked, each of those is put into words on the move it is about, and what is threatened is drawn on the board. The engine decides what is said and the model only words it: every sentence is checked against the analysis before it is kept, and one the analysis does not bear out is left out rather than shown. With seven men or fewer on the board the endgame tablebase decides the result, not the engine's number.

Comments in chooses the language the comments are written in, and the choice is kept for the next study. They are still written in English first, because that is the language the check reads; only the sentences that pass it are translated, and the translation is checked as well — every move, square and move number must come back exactly as it was, and moves keep their English letters. A sentence whose translation does not pass is left out rather than shown in English, and the end of the study says how many were. The translation adds some seconds to the wait and uses no more of your monthly studies.

Open as a tutorial takes the finished study to the Tutorial Studio, one line to a part, with its comments and its arrows. A new tutorial is marked as written in the language of the comments, so its film is spoken in it; a tutorial you were already writing keeps its own language, and says so if the study's is a different one.

The opening database

For a position early in a game, the explorer says what has actually been played there and how it turned out, counted over master games in which both players were rated 2200 or more. The book covers the first 50 plies of those games; for a position deeper than that, it says so rather than showing an empty list. You need to be signed in to see it.

The tree of what you tried

Every move you play is kept as a branch rather than replacing what was there, so trying a second idea does not lose the first. Right-click a move in the tree (on a phone, press and hold it) for its menu: Promote to Main Line makes a variation the main line, Move variation earlier and Move variation later change the order of the variations after it — the order the PGN writes them in — and Delete this variation removes one. Delete this move (and branch after it) takes back a single move with everything under it.

Words go on a move with Add Comment — later Add / Edit Comment — and NAG Symbols (!, ?) add the usual annotation marks. Generate AI comment offers a sentence about the move you are on — how it stands against the engine's own move and what follows it — if you want a starting point. It is written in the language chosen under Comments in, and says so when that is not English. It opens in the comment editor, and is yours only once you save it.

Keep it, or teach from it

Save analysis keeps the tree in your library so you can come back to it.

To turn what is on the board into teaching material, press Use in a tutorial. It offers three ways to start a new tutorial:

  • New tutorial from this position
  • New tutorial from this line (when the position has moves after it)
  • New tutorial from this game (when the tree has moves)

It also lets you add to an existing tutorial or open one to edit:

  • Add this position to a tutorial…
  • Add this line to a tutorial…
  • Open a tutorial to edit…

What arrives in the studio is a part you can write on; see writing a tutorial.

What can go wrong

  • Saving asks you to sign in — analysis is kept on the server, so a guest cannot save it.
  • A PGN will not open — Cannot read file contents. means the file is not readable text; paste the moves instead.
  • The engine says nothing — it is still starting up (Calculating moves...), or the panel is switched off in Settings.
